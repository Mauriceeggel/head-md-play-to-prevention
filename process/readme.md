## Exercise

![notes01](img/notes03.jpeg)
→ This is an example of world building that demand features <br>
(that can ultimately create "emerging harassment").

<u>Let's imagine a scenario in which gameplay mechanics can generate a situation of harassment:</u>

The slay system is, in this case, central to the functioning of the game.



“Slay points” are independent of the social class of the character being played and depend entirely on the economy governing the game. The more “money” a player has, the more slay points they accumulate.

As a result, harassers could, for example, mock players who have less money and therefore fewer slay points. Perhaps this money could be easily obtained through a pay-to-win system, causing harassers to bring the victim’s real-life economic situation into the virtual world.

By extension, a harasser could draw a parallel between the victim’s personal life and their in-game character, as well as the number of “slay points” they have.

Although using a public discussion channel can be risky for a harasser, as they may be excluded or banned from it, they could nevertheless use it to spread false rumors about the victim, potentially causing the victim to be excluded from or even banned from the server.

Similarly, one-to-one communication channels provide the harasser with a direct point of contact with the victim, allowing them to discriminate against, insult, or otherwise harass them.

The multiplayer aspect of the game can also create additional problems. It requires players to create an account, which can potentially be tracked by one or more harassers across different servers. Over time, this could ultimately prevent the victim from being able to play the game altogether.
